Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to common questions about blood donation eligibility.

I have had jaundice in the past. Can I donate blood?

It depends on the cause and timing. If you had jaundice as a newborn infant, you are fully eligible to donate. However, if you had jaundice caused by Hepatitis B or C at any point in your life, you are permanently deferred. If it was caused by something else (like gallstones or medications), you may donate once you are fully recovered.

I am a regular smoker. Can I still donate blood?

Yes, smokers can donate blood. Cigarette or tobacco use does not automatically disqualify you from donating. As long as you meet all other health and weight requirements, your blood is safe to give.

Can I smoke right before or after my donation?

You should strictly avoid smoking for at least 2 hours before your appointment and 3 hours after your donation. Smoking beforehand can increase your heart rate and blood pressure, while smoking right after increases your chances of feeling dizzy or fainting.

What are the major requirements to donate blood?

The core baseline requirements for most blood centers include:

  • Age: Generally between 18 and 65 years old.
  • Weight: Must weigh at least 110 lbs (50 kg).
  • General Health: Must feel completely well and healthy on the day of donation (no active colds or infections or any disease that would make donation unsafe).
  • Hemoglobin: Your iron levels will be checked via a quick finger-prick test at the center to ensure they are within a safe range.
